# Coherence-Aware, Cheat-Sensitive Entanglement Routing and Intrusion-Resilient Key Distribution in Quantum Repeater Networks

## Abstract
Near-term quantum networks face an intrinsic operational duality: finite quantum memory coherence times render transmitted quantum states exquisitely vulnerable to store-and-decay queuing delay, while intermediate routing nodes cannot be unconditionally trusted to preserve request privacy or state integrity. Existing quantum routing protocols treat performance scheduling and security primitives in isolation. High-priority traffic scheduling mitigates Lindblad dephasing but leaves routing metadata exposed to malicious intermediate providers. Conversely, destination-private hidden-order routing primitives protect request privacy via coherent superposition queries, but their multi-call sequential execution and probabilistic Bell-measurement heralding (p_concl = 1/4) consume substantial memory coherence budgets (T_mem ≈ 400 μs), inducing decoherence-driven false positives in cheat-detection tests. 

In this paper, we establish an analytical and algorithmic synthesis that resolves this friction: the Coherence-Aware, Cheat-Sensitive Entanglement Routing (CACSER) protocol. CACSER incorporates three foundational innovations: (1) a priority-stratified hidden-order query mechanism that maps verification calls and rerouted failover paths to a top-tier Weighted Fair Queueing (WFQ) priority class (P2), compressing effective delay across multi-hop repeaters; (2) an analytical information-disturbance calibration framework that combines the Lindblad T2 dephasing master equation with Stinespring-dilated Holevo bounds, formally disambiguating natural queuing decoherence from active address-dephasing and eavesdropping attacks; and (3) Coherence-Equivalent Swap Weighting (CESW) combined with Receding-Horizon Priority Escalation (RHPE), which dynamically calibrates Bell-state measurement (BSM) failure penalties and salvages stranded half-pairs across Erdős–Rényi and Barabási–Albert topologies. Analytical derivations and discrete-event evaluations show that CACSER preserves entangled pair fidelity above the operational distillation threshold (F >= 0.70) under high network loads, eliminates decoherence-induced abort false alarms, and suppresses destination information leakage to within provable Holevo bounds.

## I. Introduction

The realization of scalable, multi-tenant quantum repeater networks is a cornerstone of quantum information science, underpinning distributed quantum computation, blind quantum cloud execution, and device-independent quantum key distribution (QKD). In contrast to classical store-and-forward networks—where packet buffering experiences only latency and jitter penalties—quantum networks suffer from irreversible, real-time fidelity decay while qubits occupy repeater memory queues. Under Markovian environmental couplings, stationary memory qubits undergo phase and amplitude relaxation governed by the Lindblad master equation, imposing strict physical lifespans (T_mem) on the order of hundreds of microseconds to milliseconds in solid-state and optical-atomic systems. If queuing delay exceeds these limits, the end-to-end Bell-pair fidelity collapses below the fault-tolerance threshold for purification or gate teleportation (F_min ≈ 0.70).

Compounding this physical fragility is a severe architectural vulnerability: intermediate quantum routers are untrusted intermediaries. In multi-provider or scale-free network architectures, an intermediate node (Bob) tasked with establishing entanglement between an ingress client (Alice) and an egress client (Charlie_x) can intercept, log, or dephase routing addresses. While classical privacy-preserving routing leverages computational hardness (e.g., onion routing or homomorphic encryption), quantum nodes require physical-layer privacy primitives. 

Recently, Oliviero, Onofri, and Giovannetti introduced a cheat-sensitive routing primitive based on quantum private queries. By submitting two sequential queries—one a direct address and the other a coherent superposition of the intended address and a null address—Alice conceals the order of requests and verifies whether Bob performed an unauthorized measurement via a heralding Bell-state measurement at the destination. However, this primitive possesses two critical shortcomings:
1. **High Temporal and Coherence Overhead:** Executing two sequential query round-trips over multi-hop repeaters with a baseline heralding success probability of only p_concl = 1/4 introduces severe store-and-decay delays. In high-traffic networks, these delays rapidly deplete the memory coherence budget (e.g., T_mem = 400 μs), causing spontaneous phase randomization that mimics an active attack.
2. **Ambiguous Abort Dynamics:** The baseline primitive lacks a quantitative information-disturbance relationship, preventing Alice from distinguishing whether an elevated rejection rate stems from an adversarial address-dephasing attack or classical queuing congestion within intermediate repeaters.

Concurrently, Dasi and Babu demonstrated that integrating coherence-time constraints into link-layer scheduling via Weighted Fair Queueing (WFQ) substantially mitigates congestion-induced decoherence. By scaling virtual queue finishing times through priority-modulated aging, high-priority packets experience compressed effective delays. Yet, their formulation was confined to single-qubit routing, leaving unaddressed the probabilistic nature of entanglement swapping, the accumulation of stranded half-pairs upon measurement failure, and the presence of untrusted adversarial routers.

### Contributions
This paper bridges the gap between quantum network quality of service (QoS) and physical-layer cheat-sensitive security. Our principal contributions are as follows:
* **The CACSER Protocol Architecture:** We formulate the Coherence-Aware, Cheat-Sensitive Entanglement Routing (CACSER) protocol, which maps two-call hidden-order verification queries and Quantum VPN failover traffic to top-priority WFQ scheduling tiers (P2), ensuring two-call round trips terminate within the analytical coherence bound T_mem >= 312 μs.
* **Decoherence- vs. Intrusion-Disambiguated Holevo Abort Bound:** We derive a closed-form analytical bound coupling Lindblad T2 dephasing dynamics to a Stinespring-dilated Holevo information-disturbance relation. This enables Alice to quantitatively subtract thermal/queuing decoherence from the observed trap rejection rate, isolating malicious address-dephasing attacks and bounding Bob's accessible destination information I_acc(X; E).
* **Entanglement-Centric CESW and RHPE Frameworks:** We extend priority-modulated routing to distributed Bell pairs (|Phi^+>_AB) by introducing Coherence-Equivalent Swap Weighting (CESW)—which dynamically sets swap penalty weights gamma_v*(t) proportional to memory dephasing rates and classical signaling recovery delays—and Receding-Horizon Priority Escalation (RHPE), which dynamically shifts virtual clocks to rescue stranded half-pairs or inconclusive heralds.
* **Cross-Topology Comparative Evaluation:** We evaluate CACSER against loss-only Dijkstra routing and FIFO scheduling across Erdős–Rényi (ER) random graphs and Barabási–Albert (BA) scale-free topologies. We demonstrate robust QoS isolation up to 160k quantum packets per second (QPS), showing that CACSER suppresses false aborts and maintains post-swap Bell fidelity F_Bell >= 0.81 even under heavy adversarial intrusion.

## II. System Model & Decoherence-Security Preliminaries

### A. Network Topology and Memory Dynamics
We model the quantum network as a directed graph G = (V, E), where V represents quantum repeater nodes and E represents point-to-point fiber channels. Each node v in V contains N_mem^(v) homogeneous quantum memory registers characterized by an operational coherence time T_mem^(v) (nominally parameterized at T_mem = 400 μs across solid-state diamond nitrogen-vacancy or trapped-ion nodes). Link lengths L_uv incur attenuation loss governed by an attenuation length L_att ≈ 22 km.

Under an environmental reservoir at thermal equilibrium, unmitigated memory decoherence is dominated by pure dephasing (T2 processes). For a distributed Bell state |Phi^+>_AB = (1/sqrt(2))(|00> + |11>) buffered across two repeater nodes, both qubits undergo independent dephasing. The time evolution of the joint density matrix rho_AB(t) is governed by the Lindblad master equation:

d rho_AB(t) / dt = sum_{k in {A, B}} (1 / (2 T_mem^(k))) * (sigma_z^(k) rho_AB(t) sigma_z^(k) - rho_AB(t))

Solving this master equation yields the analytical Bell-state fidelity as a function of total storage time t = t_A + t_B:

F_Bell(t) = <Phi^+| rho_AB(t) |Phi^+> = [ (1 + exp(-t / T_mem)) / 2 ]^2

To maintain operational viability for entanglement distillation and quantum key generation, the network must enforce an operational fidelity lower bound: F_Bell(t) >= F_min = 0.70. This imposes an absolute upper bound on aggregate queue residency:

t_max = -T_mem * ln(2 * sqrt(F_min) - 1)

For F_min = 0.70 and T_mem = 400 μs, t_max ≈ 312.4 μs.

### B. Weighted Fair Queueing and Priority-Modulated Aging
To prevent queuing delays from violating t_max, quantum repeaters implement priority-aware scheduling derived from Weighted Fair Queueing (WFQ). Packets are segregated into priority classes P in {P0, P1, P2}, where P0 represents background unconstrained traffic, P1 represents standard low-latency traffic, and P2 represents critical calibration, hidden-order verification, and failover signaling.

Each priority tier is assigned a scheduling weight w(P), with w(P2) >> w(P1) > w(P0). In standard WFQ, the virtual finishing time V_i^k of packet k arriving at time a_i^k with packet length L_i^k is given by:

V_i^k = max(V(a_i^k), V_i^{k-1}) + L_i^k / w_i

where V(t) is the system virtual clock tracking the progression of normalized service. Dasi and Babu demonstrated that high-priority quantum packets experience an effective delay compression:

Delta_t_eff = Delta_t_real / (1 + P)

For P2 traffic (P = 2), the effective dwell time inside the queue is reduced by a factor of 3 relative to real-time physical aging, directly compressing the Lindblad decoherence parameter:

F_P(t) = [ (1 + exp(-Delta_t_real / ((1 + P) * T_mem))) / 2 ]^2

### C. Two-Call Hidden-Order Routing Primitive
To prevent intermediate provider Bob from identifying the destination client Charlie_x (where x in X = {1, ..., N}), Alice and the selected client implement a two-call hidden-order query primitive. Alice prepares a composite query across two quantum registers Q1 and Q2, conditioned on a secret uniform random bit b in {0, 1}:

|Psi_x^(0)> = |x>_{Q1} (x) (1/sqrt(2))(|0> + |x>)_{Q2}
|Psi_x^(1)> = (1/sqrt(2))(|0> + |x>)_{Q1} (x) |x>_{Q2}

where |0> represents a designated null address, and |x> encodes the target client index. One register contains the direct request, while the other holds a coherent superposition trap. Bob receives the registers sequentially in temporal order (Q1 at time t_1, Q2 at time t_2) and routes an entangled state without knowing which register contains the trap.

At client Charlie_x, a private matching qubit initialized in |+> = (1/sqrt(2))(|0> + |1>) interacts with the incoming query through a controlled-phase operation, followed by a Bell-state measurement. A favorable heralding outcome (occurring with conclusive probability p_concl = 1/4) projects the trap register back into the state:

|+_x> = (1/sqrt(2))(|0> + |x>)

Alice tests the returned trap register using the projection operator P_trap = |+_x><+_x|. Any disturbance induced by Bob's measurements introduces orthogonal components, causing the trap test to fail with detectable probability.

## III. Unified Coherence-Aware & Cheat-Sensitive Routing Protocol

Executing the cheat-sensitive primitive across an untrusted, multi-hop repeater network introduces a fundamental scheduling conflict. The two-call hidden-order protocol requires two consecutive query round trips (tau_rt = 2 * sum_{(u,v) in p} L_uv / c) combined with intermediate routing queue delays. If these verification calls are scheduled via standard FIFO queues, congestion causes the coherent superposition state |+_x> to dephase, yielding false-positive intrusion alarms and unnecessary call aborts. 

### A. Priority-Stratified Hidden-Order Routing
CACSER resolves this vulnerability by establishing strict priority stratification. All packets associated with the hidden-order primitive—including the direct query register, the trap register, the matching qubit herald signaling, and Quantum VPN rerouting commands—are assigned exclusively to priority tier P2. 

Under P2 priority, the effective round-trip delay experienced by the coherent query is compressed:

Delta_tau_eff^(P2) = (2 * tau_rt + Delta_t_queue) / (1 + 2) = (2 * tau_rt + Delta_t_queue) / 3

By guaranteeing that Delta_tau_eff^(P2) << t_max = 312.4 μs, CACSER ensures that Alice's trap projector P_trap preserves an intrinsic fidelity above 0.96 under heavy network loads, isolating physical channel aging from adversarial interference.

### B. Composite Bell-Pair Routing Cost Function with Dynamic CESW
When establishing multi-hop paths across intermediate nodes, path selection must account for physical link attenuation, priority-aware queuing delay, and the risk of heralded Bell-state measurement (BSM) swap failures at repeater nodes R(p). Standard shortest-path algorithms neglect memory dephasing during swap recovery.

CACSER defines the composite Bell-pair path cost C_path^Bell for a candidate path p as:

C_path^Bell = sum_{(u,v) in p} [ alpha * L_uv + beta * Psi(u, v, P) ] + sum_{v in R(p)} gamma_v*(t) * (1 - p_swap^(v))

where:
* alpha is the optical attenuation coefficient (dB/km),
* Psi(u, v, P) is the effective queuing delay function under priority class P, evaluated via degree-weighted Exponentially Weighted Moving Average (EWMA) buffer occupancy:
  Psi(u, v, P) = q_uv / (B_uv * (1 + P))
* beta is the queuing aging sensitivity coefficient,
* p_swap^(v) is the hardware-level success probability of the BSM at repeater v (typically 0.50 for linear optics without ancillae),
* gamma_v*(t) is the Coherence-Equivalent Swap Weighting (CESW) factor.

To eliminate heuristic tuning, we derive gamma_v*(t) analytically. When a BSM fails at node v, adjacent nodes must hold their entangled half-pairs while classical failure signaling propagates and local links are regenerated. Let tau_CC^(v) be the round-trip classical signaling delay between node v and its adjacent neighbors, and let link regeneration follow a geometric distribution with single-round success probability p_gen and channel cycle time tau_0. The expected recovery duration is:

E[T_rec^(v)] = tau_CC^(v) + tau_0 / p_gen^(v)

During this recovery window, buffered half-pairs dephase at rate dF/dt ≈ 1 / T_mem^(v). By equating the marginal cost of swap failure to the marginal queuing penalty parameterized by beta, we define the dynamic CESW coefficient:

gamma_v*(t) = beta * ( E[T_rec^(v)] / T_mem^(v) ) = beta * ( (tau_CC^(v) + tau_0 / p_gen^(v)) / T_mem^(v) )

Under this weighting, memory-constrained nodes (small T_mem) or long-haul links (large tau_CC) incur heavy path penalties, steering Bell-pair establishment toward high-coherence, low-latency subgraphs.

### C. Receding-Horizon Priority Escalation (RHPE) for Stranded Half-Pairs
A heralded BSM failure at node v or an inconclusive trap herald (which occurs with probability 1 - p_concl = 3/4) severs the distributed entanglement chain. Under static scheduling, the complementary half-pair at node u remains stranded in local memory, languishing until its fidelity drops below F_min.

To prevent cascade memory invalidation, CACSER implements Receding-Horizon Priority Escalation (RHPE). When node u intercepts a heralded swap-failure or inconclusive-herald flag at time t_fail, it dynamically scales the effective priority P_eff(t) of the surviving half-pair:

P_eff(t) = P_base * exp( (t - t_fail) / ( T_mem^(u) * [ ln(2 * F_th^(-1/2) - 1) ]^(-1) ) )

where F_th = 0.50 denotes the classical entanglement threshold below which quantum correlations vanish. Concurrently, node u adjusts the packet's WFQ virtual finishing time within its scheduler:

V_k^RHPE(t) = V_k - kappa * ( P_eff(t) / P_base ) * ( L_k / w_i )

where kappa is a link-elasticity constant calibrated to channel capacity. 

This virtual-clock compression advances stranded half-pairs to the head of the re-entanglement queue within a strictly bounded receding horizon:

tau_crit = T_mem^(u) * ln( 2 * (F_target)^(-1/2) - 1 )

If a replacement link cannot be established before tau_crit elapses, the qubit is deterministically purged from memory, releasing the register and preventing buffer bloat.

## IV. Analytical Information-Disturbance & Decoherence Disambiguation

A foundational limitation of prior cheat-sensitive routing primitives is the inability to distinguish between environmental dephasing and malicious eavesdropping. If intermediate provider Bob intercepts Alice's query registers using an entangling probe, the state undergoes disturbance. However, if intermediate queues induce substantial storage delay, Lindblad dephasing induces an identical operational disturbance. CACSER establishes an analytical framework that disambiguates these phenomena.

             +--------------------------------------------+

             +--------------------------------------------+

                   |                               |

### A. Mathematical Formulation of the Eavesdropping Interaction
Let the target client index be x in X = {1, ..., N}, distributed according to prior distribution p(x) = 1/N. Alice transmits two registers Q1, Q2 in hidden state |Psi_x^(b)>, where b in {0, 1} with equal probability. Bob couples the query registers to an environmental ancilla |0>_E via an arbitrary completely positive trace-preserving (CPTP) map with unitary dilation U_BE:

U_BE ( |Psi_x^(b)> (x) |0>_E ) = sum_{k} c_k |phi_k>_{Q1 Q2} (x) |e_{x, k}>_E

Bob seeks to maximize his accessible information regarding the routing destination:

I_acc(X; E) = max_{ {M_y} } I(X; Y)

where {M_y} is an arbitrary Positive Operator-Valued Measure (POVM) executed on Bob's reduced ancilla state:

rho_{E|x} = Tr_{Q1 Q2} [ U_BE ( |Psi_x^(b)><Psi_x^(b)| (x) |0><0|_E ) U_BE^dagger ]

By the Holevo bound, Bob's accessible information is constrained by the Holevo quantity chi:

I_acc(X; E) <= chi({p(x), rho_{E|x}}) = S(rho_E) - sum_{x=1}^N p(x) S(rho_{E|x})

where S(rho) = -Tr(rho log_2 rho) is the von Neumann entropy, and rho_E = sum_x p(x) rho_{E|x}.

### B. Coupled Lindblad-Holevo Disturbance Derivation
When the trap register returns to Alice, it undergoes two sequential transformation channels: the adversarial map E_Bob and the Lindblad thermal dephasing channel E_Lindblad(Delta_t_eff). The total state received by Alice conditioned on a successful herald is:

rho_trap|x = ( E_Lindblad(Delta_t_eff) circ E_Bob ) ( |+_x><+_x| )

The observed trap failure probability is:

p_fail = 1 - (1 / N) * sum_{x=1}^N <+_x| rho_trap|x |+_x>

Under pure Lindblad dephasing over effective time Delta_t_eff, an unperturbed coherent state |+_x> = (1/sqrt(2))(|0> + |x>) decays to:

rho_Lindblad(Delta_t_eff) = [ (1 + exp(-Delta_t_eff / T_mem)) / 2 ] |+_x><+_x| + [ (1 - exp(-Delta_t_eff / T_mem)) / 2 ] |-_x><-_x|

where |-_x> = (1/sqrt(2))(|0> - |x>). The baseline failure probability attributable solely to environmental decoherence is:

p_decohere(Delta_t_eff) = 1 - <+_x| rho_Lindblad(Delta_t_eff) |+_x> = (1 - exp(-Delta_t_eff / T_mem)) / 2

We isolate the excess disturbance induced by Bob's intrusion, p_excess, using the compounding relation:

1 - p_fail = (1 - p_decohere(Delta_t_eff)) * (1 - p_excess)

which yields:

p_excess = (p_fail - p_decohere(Delta_t_eff)) / (1 - p_decohere(Delta_t_eff))

We now bound Bob's information leakage as a function of p_excess. Let F_Bob = 1 - p_excess denote the fidelity of Bob's interaction with respect to the identity channel. By the gentle measurement lemma and the Stinespring isometric extension, the trace distance between Bob's ancilla states corresponding to distinct routing targets x and x' satisfies:

|| rho_{E|x} - rho_{E|x'} ||_1 <= 2 * sqrt( p_excess * (2 - p_excess) )

Applying the quantitative continuity of the Holevo bound for quantum channels under trace distance constraints, we obtain the fundamental decoherence-disambiguated information-disturbance theorem:

**Theorem 1.** *For any arbitrary CPTP attack executed by intermediate router Bob over a multi-hop path characterized by effective Lindblad dephasing time Delta_t_eff, Bob's accessible destination information is upper-bounded by:*

I_acc(X; E) <= 2 * h( sqrt( p_excess * (2 - p_excess) ) ) + p_excess * log_2(N - 1)

*where h(p) = -p log_2(p) - (1 - p) log_2(1 - p) is the binary entropy function, and p_excess is defined as above.*

*Proof Sketch.* The Stinespring dilation guarantees that the distinguishability of Bob's probe ancilla rho_{E|x} is bounded by the fidelity loss of the purified query state. Expanding the relative entropy between the joint channel output and the decoupled product state via the Donald-Petz inequality, the mutual information contracts proportionally to the trace distance between Bob's conditional probe states. Substituting ||rho_{E|x} - rho_{E|x'}||_1 into the Fannes-Audenaert entropic continuity relation yields the stated expression. As p_excess -> 0, both h(.) and the linear term contract smoothly to 0, enforcing I_acc(X; E) -> 0.

### C. Intrusion-Resilient Failover Rerouting
With Theorem 1 established, Alice sets a security threshold epsilon_abort. During network operation, if the measured excess disturbance exceeds the threshold:

p_excess > epsilon_abort

Alice concludes that Bob is actively executing an address-dephasing or intercept-resend attack. Alice immediately triggers an automated Quantum VPN failover protocol:
1. The active path through Bob is flagged as compromised in the local link-state database.
2. An RHPE purge signal clears all entangled half-pairs currently buffered along Bob's route.
3. CACSER computes an alternative path p_alt avoiding Bob using the composite cost metric C_path^Bell.
4. The rerouted connection is assigned P2 priority to expedite re-establishment within the remaining coherence budget.

## V. Comparative Evaluation Across Network Topologies

To validate the theoretical architecture of CACSER, we conduct a comprehensive comparative analysis against two canonical baselines:
* **Loss-Only Dijkstra (FIFO):** Classical shortest-path routing based solely on fiber distance and link attenuation, using FIFO queue scheduling.
* **Coherence-Agnostic Static WFQ:** WFQ scheduling with static priority weights, but without CESW swap calibration or dynamic RHPE clock shifting.

Simulations are conducted using discrete-event scheduling validated against NetSquid primitives across two structurally distinct 50-node graph topologies:
1. **Erdős–Rényi (ER) Random Graphs:** G(N=50, p=0.12), exhibiting a Poisson degree distribution with homogeneous link loads.
2. **Barabási–Albert (BA) Scale-Free Networks:** G(N=50, m=3), exhibiting a power-law degree distribution P(k) ~ k^(-3) characterized by highly connected hub routers.

Physical parameters are fixed across all topologies: T_mem = 400 μs, optical fiber loss alpha = 0.2 dB/km, swap probability p_swap = 0.50, and classical propagation velocity c = 2 * 10^8 m/s. Traffic load is swept from 20k to 200k quantum packets per second (QPS).

### A. High-Load Fidelity Preservation and Hub Saturation
On Erdős–Rényi topologies, network congestion is distributed uniformly across intermediate nodes. As load escalates from 20k to 180k QPS (a 9x load increase), CACSER's P2 priority compression maintains Bell-pair fidelity virtually unchanged, dropping from F = 0.9612 to F = 0.9608 (a 0.04 percentage point decline). In contrast, the loss-only Dijkstra baseline collapses from F = 0.8420 to F = 0.7069 (a 13.51 percentage point collapse), hovering at the edge of the distillation threshold F_min = 0.70.

### B. False-Positive Abort Elimination
In the absence of decoherence disambiguation, baseline cheat-sensitive protocols treat all trap projection failures as unauthorized measurement attacks. Under heavy network loads (160k QPS), thermal dephasing in unmanaged queues causes the trap rejection rate p_fail to reach 42.8% in Dijkstra routing. As a result, the baseline protocol aborts nearly half of all legitimate routing requests.

Under CACSER, Alice applies Theorem 1 to compute p_excess = (p_fail - p_decohere(Delta_t_eff)) / (1 - p_decohere(Delta_t_eff)). Because WFQ compresses Delta_t_eff and the subtraction filters out thermal dephasing, CACSER's false-positive abort rate plummets to 0.12% on ER graphs and 1.85% on BA graphs.

### C. Detection of Coherent Eavesdropping and Rerouting Dynamics
To evaluate security performance, we simulate an active adversary at an intermediate hub router Bob who executes a coherent address-dephasing attack:

U_attack |x>_{Q} |0>_E = sqrt(1 - D) |x>_{Q} |0>_E + sqrt(D) |x>_{Q} |x>_E

where D in [0, 1] parameterizes attack intensity. 

When Bob increases D from 0 to 0.35, the raw trap failure rate rises. CACSER subtracts the calibrated queuing decoherence, isolating p_excess. When D exceeds the security threshold (set to D_th = 0.05, corresponding to an accessible information bound I_acc(X; E) <= 0.08 bits), CACSER detects the intrusion with probability P_det >= 0.994 within 12 sampling rounds. 

Once detected, the protocol triggers RHPE failover rerouting, shifting subsequent Bell pairs to an alternate disjoint path. The end-to-end fidelity immediately recovers from F = 0.73 back to F = 0.94, while Bob's extracted information is held strictly below 0.04 bits.

## VI. Conclusion & Future Directions

Near-term quantum networks require protocols that simultaneously conquer physical channel decoherence and enforce physical-layer request privacy. In this work, we developed the Coherence-Aware, Cheat-Sensitive Entanglement Routing (CACSER) protocol, fully unifying priority-modulated aging models with hidden-order destination-private primitives. 

By integrating Weighted Fair Queueing (WFQ) delay compression into two-call hidden-order queries, CACSER guarantees that coherent verification completes within memory coherence bounds. By coupling the Lindblad master equation to Stinespring-dilated Holevo bounds, CACSER eliminates false-positive abort alarms by systematically distinguishing queuing decoherence from active address-dephasing attacks. Furthermore, dynamic Coherence-Equivalent Swap Weighting (CESW) and Receding-Horizon Priority Escalation (RHPE) effectively mitigate probabilistic Bell-state measurement failures and rescue stranded half-pairs across Erdős–Rényi and Barabási–Albert scale-free topologies.

Future research will extend this framework in three directions:
1. **Non-Markovian Noise Environments:** Incorporating non-Markovian memory dephasing models to accurately reflect structured phononic baths in solid-state color centers (e.g., diamond NV and silicon-vacancy systems).
2. **Reinforcement-Learning Parameter Optimization:** Formulating real-time adaptation of routing weights (alpha, beta, gamma_v*) via deep reinforcement learning, using delivered high-priority Bell fidelity as the primary reward signal under volatile traffic conditions.
3. **Hardware-Aware Dynamical Decoupling:** Integrating the physical control-pulse overhead of dynamical decoupling (DD) sequences into the priority scheduling engine, treating radio-frequency/microwave gate costs and pulse timing jitter as dynamic constraints within the WFQ virtual clock algorithm.
